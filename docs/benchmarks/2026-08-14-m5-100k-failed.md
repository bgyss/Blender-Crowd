## Diagnosis

### How the metrics moved from 10K to 100K

| Measure | 10K | 100K | ratio |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| median travel time | 550 s | 1,746 s | 3.17 |
| S2 stall episodes per stalled agent | 1.46 | 4.08 | 2.79 |
| S2 ticks per stall episode | 65.1 | 143.1 | 2.20 |
| S2 `stall_agent_ticks_per_agent_tick` | 5.87e-4 | 2.87e-3 | 4.89 |
| S2 `heading_reversals_per_agent_tick` | 0.0389 | 0.0404 | 1.04 |
| S2 `penetration_agent_ticks_per_agent_tick` | 6.55e-7 | 9.30e-7 | 1.42 |

Route length scales with the square root of population, so a 100K agent travels
3.2x as far as a 10K one. Stall *episodes per agent* grew 2.8x — almost exactly
that ratio — so agents are blocked at a near-constant rate per metre travelled.
Episodes also became 2.2x longer, which is the part that indicates jams
persisting rather than merely occurring more often.

Median travel time is within 3% of free-flow for the route length, so the crowd
is not globally congested; the failures come from localised, transient events.

### A hypothesis that was tested and refuted

Spawn placement was the obvious suspect: `place_clear_of_others` gives up after
24 rejection attempts and places the agent anyway, and 100K draws ten times as
often as 10K. `crates/crowd-core/tests/m5_spawn_penetration_probe.rs` measured
it directly:

| population | overlap when emission completes | worst in first 400 ticks | worst over the full run |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 10,000 | 0.000000 m | 0.000000 m | 0.018272 m |
| 100,000 | 0.000000 m | 0.006531 m | 0.135480 m |

The placer emits a cleanly separated crowd at both scales. The deep overlaps
develop over the route, so they are congestion behaviour. The probe is kept so
this theory cannot be re-proposed without evidence.

## Optimisation round

### Accepted: hoisting candidate-independent work out of the solver

`side_bias_cost` ran once per candidate velocity, but its head-on test and
per-neighbour bearing — two square roots each — do not depend on the candidate,
and were being recomputed for all ~50 of them. Stubbing the function out
entirely measured a 1.31x whole-simulation speedup, bounding the opportunity.
Hoisting the candidate-independent part into `SideBiasCache` recovered 1.22x
(663 to 810 ticks/s at 1,000 agents) with an **identical** `final_state_hash`.

### Accepted: parallel steering

Steering is 70% of measured phase time and each agent's solution is a pure
function of the previous-tick snapshot. The phase is now split: a compute pass
that takes `&World` and writes per-slot outcomes, optionally across threads, and
an apply pass that walks slots in order. Both the world writes and the float
reduction stay in the original sequence, so the parallel and sequential paths
agree bitwise rather than approximately.

Measured at 10,000 agents: **60.14 to 197.0 ticks/s, 3.28x** (an earlier
measurement of the same change read 185.0; the spread is run-to-run noise), with
an identical `final_state_hash` — 16004330017290778013 both before and after —
and an identical penetration count of 49. All six checked-in legacy
baselines still report OK, and `parallel_and_sequential_steering_agree_bitwise`
runs the same scene both ways and requires exact agreement.

### Rejected: a coarse solver for the background tier

M5's fidelity model suggests giving background agents a cheaper representation,
so a preset halving the heading ring was measured. At 1,000 agents it bought
1.57x throughput but regressed contact quality past the gate: S2 penetration
rate 4.3e-7 to 1.4e-5 and peak depth 0.00087 m to 0.0576 m, failing three checks
the full-resolution solver passes with wide headroom.

Angular sampling resolution converts directly into contact quality, and quality
is the scarce resource at this scale. The preset remains available through
`Simulation::set_background_solver` for callers who want that trade explicitly;
it is **not** enabled for the scale profile.

## The two quality failures need a decision, not a threshold edit

The throughput failure is unambiguous and the work above addresses it. The two
quality failures raise a question about the threshold file that should be
settled deliberately.

`benchmarks/thresholds/m5-city-flow.json` claims its limits are scale-invariant
because they are rates per observed agent-tick. **Two of them are not rates.**

- `stalled_agent_share` is a *lifetime cumulative probability*. Even at a
  perfectly constant blocking rate per metre, it must rise toward 1.0 as route
  length grows. A fixed fraction cannot hold across scales that change route
  length.
- `max_penetration_depth_m` is an *extremum over samples*. Its expected value
  grows with sample count, and 100K draws 32x more agent-ticks than 10K.

Every genuinely rate-shaped limit passed. That does **not** by itself mean the
run is fine: `stall_agent_ticks_per_agent_tick` is rate-shaped and still grew
4.89x, passing only on headroom. So there is real degradation as well as a
specification defect, and the two must not be used to excuse each other.

Three options, none of which should be taken silently:

1. **Re-specify the two metrics** in scale-invariant form — stall episodes per
   agent-metre, and report peak depth without gating it (or gate it against a
   sample-count-aware bound) — and keep both reported. Honest about the defect,
   but it converts failing checks into passing ones and must be reviewed as
   such.
2. **Reduce jam persistence in the solver.** The density feedback
   `1/(1 + 0.18 x crowding)` has no floor, so dense clusters slow toward a crawl
   and clear slowly. A minimum-progress floor is physically motivated. It
   changes every scene, so it invalidates all six checked-in baselines and
   requires re-validating the M0–M4 evidence that rests on them.
3. **Widen the fixture's lanes** so overtaking is possible at 2.66 m pitch.
   This changes what the fixture tests and, done in response to a failed gate,
   is hard to distinguish from moving the goalposts.

My reading is that 1 and 2 are both warranted and 3 is not, but this is a
contract decision rather than an implementation one.

## What this report does not establish

- No 100K headline follows from this run. It failed.
- The optimisation numbers above are measured at 1K and 10K only. **No 100K run
  of the optimised code exists.** Two attempts to produce one from the agent
  session were killed by its execution window (the original 7.5-hour failing run
  succeeded because it was launched from a normal terminal). Any 100K figure
  quoted from the 3.28x speedup is a projection — roughly 17 ticks/s — and must
  not be reported as a measurement until `scripts/m5-100k-gate.sh` has been run
  to completion in a durable session.
- Blender evidence was deliberately not gathered: it supports a passing gate.
- Nothing here changes the accepted [10K report](2026-08-14-m5-10k.md), whose
  results are reproduced bitwise by the optimised code.
